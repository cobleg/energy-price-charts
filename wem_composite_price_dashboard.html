Composite Price — Energy + Capacity Components (2012 – 2026)

The composite price adds the annualised Reserve Capacity cost per MWh (long-run capital cost recovery) on top of the monthly mean market price (short-run marginal cost). The capacity component steps up or down each October when a new capacity year begins and a new Reserve Capacity Price takes effect.

Methodology & Notes

Two Market Eras — One Price Series

This dashboard spans two distinct WEM market structures:

  • Balancing Market (Jul 2012 – Sep 2023): AEMO dispatched scheduled generators via the Balancing Merit Order. The Balancing Price cleared the market each 30-minute interval against non-scheduled (wind/solar) supply and forecast demand. Price floor was typically −$1,000/MWh; cap was $300/MWh rising to $1,018/MWh by 2022.
  • WEM DE — Reference Trading Price (Oct 2023 – Apr 2026): The new market structure uses a Real-Time Market with 5-minute dispatch. The Reference Trading Price is a time-weighted average of Market Clearing Prices per 30-minute interval. The price cap is $1,000/MWh; floor varies.
  • Both price series are monthly means of the 30-minute interval clearing prices.

The generation metric also differs: Balancing Market total generation (scheduled + non-scheduled, MW) reflects all grid-connected output; WEM DE sent-out generation from tt30gen measures commercial facilities only. Both are converted to MWh per 30-minute interval (MW × 0.5) before summing.

Composite Price — Capacity Cost per MWh

  • Step 1 — Total Annual Capacity Cost: Peak RCP ($/MW/yr) × Total Capacity Credits (MW).
  • Step 2 — Annual Sent-Out Energy: Sum of monthly gen_sum (MWh) for all 12 months in the capacity year (October to September). Each month's gen_sum is normalised to a full-month equivalent: (gen_sum / gen_count) × (days_in_month × 48). Partial years are annualised by scaling available months to 12.
  • Step 3: Cost/MWh = Total Annual Cost ÷ Annual MWh.
  • Composite Price = Market Price + Capacity Cost/MWh.

Note: Formally, capacity costs are recovered from Market Customers proportional to their Individual Reserve Capacity Requirement (peak consumption share), not total generation. The $/MWh figures shown are indicative system-wide averages, not charges to any specific participant.

Reserve Capacity Price Types

  • Peak Reserve Capacity Price: Primary price for Peak Capacity Credits. Based on the BRCP (ERA-determined annualised cost of a new-entrant OCGT or battery). Ranges from ~60%–160% of BRCP depending on capacity surplus/scarcity.
  • Peak Transitional Price: A protected price band (floor and ceiling, CPI-adjusted annually) for Transitional Facilities — existing generators that held Capacity Credits in the 2018 Reserve Capacity Cycle. The band (initially $114,000–$140,000/MW) ensures these facilities receive a predictable revenue stream for up to 10 years, insulating them from sharp price movements during the reform period. New entrants are ineligible.
  • Flexible Reserve Capacity Price (from 2027‑28): New product for highly-responsive facilities (batteries, fast-start gas). Based on a separate Flexible BRCP reflecting battery storage costs.
  • Capacity Credits: MW of certified dispatchable capacity assigned by AEMO per facility. The total represents system-wide registered reserve capacity.
