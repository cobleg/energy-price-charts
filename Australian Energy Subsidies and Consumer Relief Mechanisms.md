

| Government Level | Name of Subsidy / Intervention | Target Consumers | Description of Subsidy |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Commonwealth Government | Energy Bill Relief Fund | Electricity Consumers (Households & Small Businesses) | Introduced to ease cost-of-living pressures, this fund provided a $1.5 billion program for eligible households in 2023–24, and was expanded to $3.5 billion in 2024–25. It provides electricity bill rebates of $300 for all Australian households and $325 for eligible small businesses, paid in quarterly instalments. |
| WA State Government | Below-Cost Regulated Retail Tariffs | Electricity Consumers (SWIS Residential & Small Business) | The WA Government has historically intervened to set regulated retail electricity tariffs (for customers of the state-owned retailer Synergy) at levels well below the actual cost of supply, acting as a direct subsidy to consumers and preserving a concentrated retail sector. |
| WA State Government | Tariff Equalisation Fund (Uniform Tariff Policy) | Electricity Consumers (Regional & Remote WA) | A mechanism designed to support a uniform tariff policy across the state. The fund subsidises the higher costs of supplying electricity to regional areas (serviced by the Regional Power Corporation / Horizon Power) by drawing financial contributions from the South West Interconnected System (SWIS) networks. This ensures remote customers pay the same regulated retail tariffs as those in the SWIS. |
| WA State Government | Feed-in Tariffs (FiTs) / Export Reward Tariffs | Electricity Consumers (Rooftop Solar Owners) | Programs that pay residential customers for exporting excess solar electricity to the grid. WA previously offered highly subsidized, premium feed-in tariffs (up to 40–60c/kWh to recover capital costs). Current structures like the Distributed Energy Buyback Scheme (DEBS) offer time-of-export subsidies, rewarding evening exports (\~10c/kWh) more than daytime exports (\~2-3c/kWh). |
| WA State Government | Domestic Gas Reservation Policy | Gas Consumers (Industrial, Commercial & Residential) | While primarily a supply mandate, requiring LNG exporters to reserve 15% of production for the domestic market, economic analysis shows this policy acts as an implicit tax on production and an implicit subsidy on domestic gas consumption. By forcing gas into the local market, it subsidises domestic consumers by artificially maintaining local prices below international export parity. |
| WA State Government | State-based Energy Concession Schemes | Electricity & Gas Consumers (Low-Income & Hardship) | In addition to federal relief, the WA government provides ongoing state-based rebate and concession schemes to help vulnerable and low-income residential customers manage their energy bills. |
