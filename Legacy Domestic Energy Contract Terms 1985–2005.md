### Term of contract

The sales contracts were set to a **20-year term**. The original domestic "take-or-pay" contracts were scheduled to run from **1985 to 2005** \[1\].

Primary buyer

The primary buyer for the domestic "take-or-pay" gas contracts was the **State Energy Commission of Western Australia (SECWA)**. SECWA's largest third-party customer was **Alcoa**, which ultimately committed to purchasing over half of the gas piped to the South West of the State (approximately 150 MMCFD).

Contracted Volume

The total contracted volume of the sales agreements signed in September 1980 was **370 million cubic feet per day (MMCFD)**, which equates to up to 10.9 million cubic metres of gas a day. This volume was amassed through a series of commitments: an initial 250 MMCFD outlined in a November 1977 Memorandum of Understanding, an additional 50 MMCFD option exercised in 1978, and a final 70 MMCFD assumed in 1979, originally intended by the joint venturers for the Pilbara market. Under the original terms, SECWA was obligated to draw down 351 MMCFD, which represented 95% of the total contracted volume.

Contracted Price

The exact price of the gas was **never made public due to strict confidentiality clauses in** the domestic gas contracts. However, the pricing structure was known to be **indexed to competing fuels**, utilising coal-competitive and oil-competitive gas pricing formulas.

Source \[2\] states:

“...Australian domestic gas prices have been relatively low. Until recently, upstream prices were around $2–3 per gigajoule in Western Australia and $3–4 per gigajoule on the east coast. In contrast, US gas prices (an indicator of gas prices globally) peaked at over US$12 per gigajoule in mid–2008…” 

### Acronyms

MMCFD: **Million Cubic Feet Per Day** (MMCFD) is a unit of measurement used to quantify the volume of natural gas.
